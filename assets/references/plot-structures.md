# Plot Structure Templates

Common story structure templates that can be used to plan novel chapters.

## Three-Act Structure

The most classic story structure, suitable for most short-to-medium novels.

### Act 1: Setup (approx. 25%)

| Chapters | Content |
|-----|------|
| Chapter 1 | Introduce the protagonist's daily life (status quo) |
| Chapter 2 | Inciting Incident - An event that disrupts the status quo |
| Chapter 3 | The protagonist refuses the call, but eventually decides to act |

**Act 1 Suspense Examples:**
- "The door opened, and the person who walked in was someone who had died three years ago."
- "The letter she received was signed with her own name."

### Act 2: Confrontation (approx. 50%)

| Chapters | Content |
|-----|------|
| Chapters 4-5 | The protagonist enters a new world and encounters challenges |
| Chapter 6 | Midpoint - A major turn or information reveal |
| Chapters 7-8 | Difficulties escalate; allies might betray |

**Act 2 Suspense Examples:**
- "The mentor turns out to be the mastermind."
- "The only ally has gone missing."

### Act 3: Resolution (approx. 25%)

| Chapters | Content |
|-----|------|
| Chapter 9 | All hope seems lost, the lowest point |
| Chapter 10 | Climax - The final showdown |
| Epilogue | Resolution, showing the new normal |

## Hero's Journey

Joseph Campbell's classic mythological structure, suitable for adventure/fantasy genres.

### Phase Breakdown

| Phase | Chapters | Content |
|-----|------|------|
| 1. Ordinary World | Chapter 1 | Introduce the protagonist's daily life |
| 2. Call to Adventure | Chapters 1-2 | Inciting incident occurs |
| 3. Refusal of the Call | Chapter 2 | The protagonist initially hesitates |
| 4. Meeting the Mentor | Chapters 2-3 | Receives guidance or equipment |
| 5. Crossing the Threshold | Chapter 3 | Leaves the comfort zone |
| 6. Tests, Allies, Enemies | Chapters 4-5 | Explores the new world |
| 7. Approach to the Inmost Cave | Chapters 5-6 | Prepares to face the major challenge |
| 8. The Ordeal | Chapters 6-7 | Approaches death or major failure |
| 9. The Reward | Chapters 7-8 | Gains power or critical information |
| 10. The Road Back | Chapter 8 | Path back is blocked |
| 11. Resurrection | Chapter 9 | Final test/transformation |
| 12. Return with the Elixir | Chapter 10 | Returns home with harvest/boon |

## Mystery Structure

Suitable for detective, mystery, and thriller genres.

### Act 1: Setup of the Puzzle

| Chapters | Content | Suspense Hook |
|-----|------|---------|
| Chapter 1 | Discovering the body / incident occurs | Who did it? |
| Chapter 2 | Detective takes on the case | Why is this case special? |
| Chapter 3 | Preliminary investigation reveals clues | Who do the clues point to? |

### Act 2: Deepening Investigation

| Chapters | Content | Suspense Hook |
|-----|------|---------|
| Chapter 4 | Interrogating suspects; everyone has suspicion | Who is lying? |
| Chapter 5 | New clue points in an unexpected direction | Were we wrong from the very start? |
| Chapter 6 | A second incident occurs, pattern emerges | Is this a serial case? |
| Chapter 7 | Detective falls into danger | Will the detective become the next target? |

### Act 3: Reveal of Truth

| Chapters | Content | Suspense Hook |
|-----|------|---------|
| Chapter 8 | Major breakthrough / twist | Is there a problem with the person we trusted? |
| Chapter 9 | Final confrontation | What is the truth? What is the cost? |
| Chapter 10 | Case resolved, lingering questions | Is it really over? |

## Romance Structure

Suitable for romance/love story genres.

### Act 1: The Meet

| Chapters | Content | Emotional Beat |
|-----|------|---------|
| Chapter 1 | Introduce protagonist A, status quo/problems | Reader empathizes with A |
| Chapter 2 | Introduce protagonist B, status quo/problems | Reader empathizes with B |
| Chapter 3 | A and B meet (poor first impression) | Destined to be together, but currently in conflict |

### Act 2: Development

| Chapters | Content | Emotional Beat |
|-----|------|---------|
| Chapters 4-5 | Forced together, getting to know each other | Discovering points of attraction |
| Chapter 6 | Attraction grows, intimate moment | Drawing closer |
| Chapters 7-8 | Misunderstandings, secrets, or obstacles emerge | Pushing apart |
| Chapter 9 | Crisis, relationship breaks down | Seeming hopeless |

### Act 3: Reconciliation

| Chapters | Content | Emotional Beat |
|-----|------|---------|
| Chapter 10 | Realizing true love, overcoming obstacles | Climax |
| Epilogue | Together, showing the new life | Happy ending / fulfillment |

## Thriller / Action Structure

Suitable for fast-paced, high-tension genres.

### Short Structure (5-6 chapters)

```text
Chapter 1: Crisis emerges → Hook: Protagonist is hunted or threatened
Chapter 2: Response plan → Hook: Plan fails, situation worsens
Chapter 3: Chase / confrontation → Hook: Forced into a corner
Chapter 4: Opportunity for turnaround → Hook: Discovers new hope, but time is tight
Chapter 5: Final confrontation → Hook: Life or death instant
Chapter 6: Aftermath → Showing the consequences
```

### Features

- Fast-paced, action in every chapter.
- High event density.
- Continuous time pressure.
- Escalating suspense intensity.

---

## Twist-Based Structure

Suitable for psychological thriller and mystery genres.

### Chapter Distribution

```text
Chapters 1-2: Establish the "apparent" situation
Chapters 3-4: Doubts emerge, but are subtle
Chapters 5-6: First minor twist (re-evaluating what is real)
Chapters 7-8: Second twist (twist upon twist)
Chapters 9-10: Final twist (everything is overturned)
```

### Key Guidelines

- Plant seemingly innocent clues early on.
- Each twist must be logical (looking back, there were signs).
- Avoid Deus Ex Machina.

## Multi-Thread Narrative Structure

Suitable for complex plots with multiple protagonists.

### Cross-Cutting Pattern

```text
Chapter 1: Protagonist A's story
Chapter 2: Protagonist B's story
Chapter 3: Protagonist A's story (advances)
Chapter 4: Protagonist B's story (advances)
Chapter 5: Threads converge
...
```

### Timeline Pattern

```text
Chapter 1: Present (Time A)
Chapter 2: Past (Time A - 5 years)
Chapter 3: Present (Time A + 1 day)
Chapter 4: Past (Time A - 5 years + 1 month)
...
```

### Convergence Principles

- All threads must eventually converge.
- Seemingly unrelated events in the early stage must be connected later.
- Convergence should produce an "so that's how it is" effect.

## Quick Structures for Short Stories

### 3-Chapter Micro Structure

```text
Chapter 1: Inciting incident + decision to act
Chapter 2: Attempt + failure + escalation
Chapter 3: Final attempt + success/failure + resolution
```

### 5-Chapter Standard Structure

```text
Chapter 1: Status quo + inciting incident
Chapter 2: Refusal + crossing the threshold
Chapter 3: Challenges + allies/enemies
Chapter 4: Low point + awakening
Chapter 5: Climax + resolution
```

## Chapter-Level Plot Templates

### Single Chapter Internal Structure

```text
Opening (10%): Hook + context connection
Development (60%): Event progression + conflict
Climax (20%): Highest point of the chapter
Ending (10%): Suspense hook + setup for next chapter
```

### Ineffective Chapter Structure (Avoid)

```text
Opening: Long setup / background exposition
Middle: Daily routine / conversational ledger
Ending: Flat ending / no suspense
```

## Structure Selection Guide

| Story Type | Recommended Structure | Number of Chapters |
|---------|---------|--------|
| Adventure/Fantasy | Hero's Journey | 8-12 chapters |
| Detective/Mystery | Mystery Structure | 8-10 chapters |
| Romance | Romance Structure | 6-10 chapters |
| Action/Thriller | Thriller Structure | 5-8 chapters |
| Psychological/Twist | Twist Structure | 6-8 chapters |
| Multi-Protagonist | Multi-Thread | 10-15 chapters |
| Micro Fiction | 3-Chapter Structure | 3 chapters |
